## 4. AI and Mediation Involvement Summary

**Was any mediated output incorporated into this piece?**
[ ] No — direct human making only
[x] **Yes**

The article's middle sections were composed across seven drafts by an AI
system working to the Architect's direction, between his own opening and
closing. He evaluated each draft; four of his redirections were
substantial enough to be recorded as separate conception anchors in the
internal chain.

**A second, separate AI system was commissioned to critique the draft
before publication**, with access to primary legal sources the drafting
session could not reach. It returned six findings. Four were factual
corrections to unsupported claims and were applied immediately. The
Architect accepted the remaining substantive findings and declined one,
which he later resolved.

**Its most consequential finding was against the article, not against
the post the article answers.** The draft had claimed the EU AI Act
could not have written its logging provisions without first answering
who holds authority. That was an overreach: the Regulation allocates
legal roles and duties, which is narrower. The article was criticising
someone for reading too much into two of its provisions while doing the
same thing itself. The published version states the narrower claim and
says so.

**The internal record classifies that critique's contribution as
AI-originated content the Architect recognised and adopted**, not as his
own insight. Adopting a correction is an act of judgment; it is not the
same as having had the thought.
